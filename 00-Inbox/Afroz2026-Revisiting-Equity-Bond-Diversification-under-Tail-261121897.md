# Revisiting Equity-Bond Diversification under Tail Risk and Structural Market Change: Evidence from Global ETFs and Model-Implied Option Prices

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
This study examines thirty equity--bond and multi-asset exchange-traded funds (ETFs) listed in the United States, Canada, and Australia using daily U.S.-dollar returns over 2020--2024. A rolling-window framework compares minimum-variance and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) portfolio optimization under long-only and long--short constraints against an equally weighted benchmark. Performance is assessed using risk-adjusted measures, downside risk, extreme-tail estimation, and conditional volatility models. Optimized portfolios exhibit lower empirical VaR and CVaR than the benchmark, with long--short strategies generally providing greater downside-risk reduction. However, the equally weighted benchmark achieves the highest reported Sharpe, Sortino, and Rachev ratios. GARCH and FIGARCH comparisons favor GARCH based on information criteria. A historically estimated Normal Inverse Gaussian model further generates European option prices and model-implied volatility surfaces under a martingale-corrected pricing specification. The findings highlight trade-offs between risk reduction, risk-adjusted performance, and practical implementation costs.
