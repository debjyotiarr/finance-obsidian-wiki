# The Information Content of News Embeddings

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
Does the predictive success of news embeddings reflect a better measure of news surprises or other sources of return predictability in text? We decompose news embeddings by content into event-category, firm-identity, and idiosyncratic components, and by timing into historical levels and current deviations. At short horizons, event categories account for most of the predictive power of embedding forecasts, and simple forecasts based on historical average returns after news in each event category outperform them. At longer horizons, firm information already present in earlier news coverage accounts for a substantial share of return predictability, even after residualization on firm characteristics. The dominance of event categories persists, and historical levels still contribute in the first months, when using a language model pretrained entirely before our sample period. For Llama-2, even embeddings constructed from news more than three years old predict returns at long horizons. However, its predictive advantage over RoBERTa is concentrated in firm identity and the historical level, and its advantage in these components largely disappears by its pretraining cutoff. A calibration shows that pretraining memory, a source of look-ahead bias, can account for this pattern. Describing news more fully is not the same as measuring news surprises, and persistent return predictability from news text does not by itself establish slow incorporation of news into prices.
