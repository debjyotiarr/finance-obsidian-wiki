# Beyond Marginal Risk: ESG and Multidimensional Fragility in Equity Markets

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
Do ESG characteristics provide financial resilience beyond conventional risk, and is that inference stable to the financial state and ESG score being measured? We study a point-in-time panel of nonfinancial S&P 500 constituents from January 2019 through November 2025. Once ex ante return, volatility, and liquidity exposures are modeled explicitly, the broad downside-resilience pattern attenuates and none of the three primary marginal ESG $\times$ Down tests survives Holm adjustment under either conventional or wild-cluster-bootstrap inference. A post-primary exact decomposition then shows that the negative Return+RVOL coefficient is almost entirely allocated to observations that also breach the CHL trading-cost tail, while the mutually exclusive Return+RVOL-only component is essentially zero. Finally, a paired standardized comparison on an identical sample shows that MSCI's downstream Industry Adjusted Score and upstream Weighted Average Key Issue Score imply materially different fitted associations in adverse market states: at a 5% market decline, the difference is about one probability percentage point per score standard deviation. Sector-time, leave- one-month-out, and observed-change timing diagnostics preserve most of this contrast, although the result remains exploratory and inference is sensitive to multiplicity and market-state support. The evidence therefore favors a narrower interpretation of ESG resilience: the apparent association weakens once conventional risk is modeled, while the remaining relation depends on both the adverse state and the MSCI score field used to represent ESG.
