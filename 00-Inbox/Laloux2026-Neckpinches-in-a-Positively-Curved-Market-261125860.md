# Neckpinches in a Positively Curved Market: Ricci Flow with Surgery on the Nikkei-225

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
We study the intrinsic geometry of the Nikkei-225 through the discrete Ollivier-Ricci curvature (ORC) of its empirical correlation graph, using the graph Ricci flow and surgery methodology of arXiv:2510.15942. The 226-node correlation graph, built from daily returns between January 2020 and June 2026, is ORC-spherical: every edge has strictly positive curvature before any flow, close to the equal-weight value $κ\approx0.502$. Under a fixed 20-iteration Ricci flow the curvature distribution polarises into a high-curvature bulk and a low-curvature minority, and cutting the low-curvature edges splits the graph into components of 184 and 42 nodes. The recursive ARCUS procedure (Adaptive Ricci Curvature Unsupervised Surgery) stops the flow at each node once a curvature gap ratio $G$ exceeds $G_{\min}$ and the surgery set is stable. With $G_{\min}=10$ and a minimum cluster size of 15, ARCUS performs 28 surgeries over 13 levels and ends in 29 terminal clusters of 2-14 nodes. The stopping rule fires at every node, with $G$ between 10.0 and 92.4, and the top-level split reproduces the fixed-schedule 184/42 partition exactly. Eight small subgraphs stop with $G<15$, so the deeper levels are conditional on $G_{\min}$. Many terminal clusters are single industries by inspection, among them nine banks, five insurers, five general trading companies, three steelmakers and three shipping lines. Two delisted stocks without price data enter as zero-correlation placeholders; with them excluded, every terminal cluster is more strongly correlated internally than with the rest of the index.
