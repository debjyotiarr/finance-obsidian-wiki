# Toward an Admissibility Theory of Aharonov--Bohm Arbitrage

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
This note develops a provisional admissibility framework for Aharonov-Bohm type arbitrage. Starting from a categorical filtration $F:\mathcal T_{\mathrm{mic}}^{\mathrm{op}}\to \mathsf{Prob}$ and its distortion on microscopic histories, we ask when a favorable closed microscopic loop can be realized as an executable relative ordinal arbitrage over an open macro-time interval. The history endofunctor $\mathsf{Hist}$ retains the staged decomposition of economic activities, while the twisted-arrow category $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathsf{Hist}(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{mic}}))$ represents a microscopic loop as an arrow between distinct histories. An observable clock $Π: \mathsf{Econ} \to \mathcal T_{\mathrm{mac}}$ and a microscopic realization functor $J: \mathsf{Hist}(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{mac}}) \to \operatorname{Tw}(\mathsf{Hist}(\mathcal T_{\mathrm{mic}}))$ connect economic histories with their microscopic geometry. Executability is treated separately through configuration histories, capacity constraints, observations, and policies. This yields pathwise and almost-sure notions of execution. A finite foreign-exchange model illustrates stochastic execution, capacity failure, terminal settlement, and comparison with a benchmark strategy. A parameterized finite filtration is constructed whose nontrivial statewise distortion agrees exactly with the relative FX settlement multiplier. Hence the resulting filtration--policy model satisfies the bridge $V_*(π^q)=dF(γ)V_*(π^p),$ and, under order compatibility, realizes relative ordinal arbitrage. The remaining problem is to identify the filtration parameters from independent market primitives and to study stability under refinement.
