# EWMA Principal Eigenportfolios of Equity Returns: Market Representation and Beta-Hedging Performance

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
This study examines whether an adaptively estimated principal eigenportfolio improves equity beta hedging relative to capitalisation-weighted and equal-weighted market benchmarks. Using stock returns from a historically changing S&P 500 universe, it constructs a portfolio from the leading principal component of an exponentially weighted second-moment matrix of volatility-standardised returns. The resulting portfolio, EWMA-PC1, is compared with SPY and RSP under a common beta-hedging procedure across randomly generated portfolios of varying sizes and weighting schemes. Evaluation considers variance reduction and average pairwise residual correlation, with paired blockbootstrap inference. EWMA-PC1 exhibits broad market participation and achieves greater mean variance reduction and lower average residual correlation than SPY across all tested configurations. Its incremental advantage over RSP is smaller and mixed. For 50-stock Dirichlet portfolios, mean variance reduction is 92.17% under PC1, compared with 79.03% under SPY and 91.93% under RSP; RSP instead achieves greater mean variance reduction for multistock liquidity-tilted portfolios. Statistical evidence of improvement is more consistent against SPY than against RSP. These findings distinguish the extraction of a market-like component from incremental hedge effectiveness and highlight the importance of benchmark weighting and targetportfolio composition. The results concern gross statistical risk reduction and exclude additional trading, financing, and borrowing costs.
