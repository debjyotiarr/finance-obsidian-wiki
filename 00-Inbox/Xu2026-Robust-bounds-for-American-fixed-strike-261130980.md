# Robust bounds for American fixed-strike lookback puts

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
We initiate the robust (model-independent) pricing of American lookback options under martingale optimal transport (MOT) constraints. Given finitely many marginals of the discounted price, we study the maximal expected discounted payoff of an American fixed-strike lookback put, whose exercise value at $t$ is $(K-\min_{u\le t}e^{ru}X_u)^+$, i.e.\ a function of the running minimum $m_t$ of the undiscounted price. Two structural facts isolate this as the non-trivial American lookback problem: at $r=0$ every classical lookback degenerates to its European counterpart, while at $r>0$ the max-based fixed-strike lookback call remains European-type, but the min-based put is genuinely American. We prove a pricing--hedging duality using the continuous-time duality of Guo--Obłoj and reducing the hedge to the two-dimensional state $(X_t,m_t)$. In the two-period model, we establish a convexity lemma and exact decomposition of the continuation payoff, deriving the exercise criterion and closed-form free-continuation value. The optimal continuation coupling is the monotone martingale coupling (the third extremal coupling), and we show that the optimal exercise region is a right tail $\{x\ge x^*\}$, in sharp contrast to the left-tail region of the standard American put. The threshold $x^*$ is monotone in parameters and affine in $K$. For finitely many marginals, the structure extends unconditionally: the optimal exercise region is always a right tail, and the optimal coupling is the reflected iterated Azéma--Yor embedding, minimizing the running minimum in stochastic order. Under the reflected Obłoj--Spoida assumption, this embedding is a two-point iterated monotone coupling; otherwise, it includes a local mass-diffusion correction. We quantify the American and model-risk premia.
