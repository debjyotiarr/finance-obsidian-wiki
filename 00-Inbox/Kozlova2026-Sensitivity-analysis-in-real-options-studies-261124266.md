# Sensitivity analysis in real options studies: systematic review and recommendations

## Abstract (verbatim from arXiv)
Real options (RO) modeling provides a powerful framework for valuing managerial flexibility under uncertainty in capital-intensive investments across various industries, including climate, energy, and transportation. However, the transparency and usefulness of such models depends heavily on sensitivity analysis (SA). This study reviews over 100 peer-reviewed RO journal articles to examine the extent and quality of SA practices. The review focuses particularly on the extent of model scrutiny: how many input variables are subjected to sensitivity analysis; whether local or global approaches are applied; and whether the shape of the input-output relationships is examined. Our findings reveal a strong overreliance on one-at-a-time (OAT) techniques and a corresponding minimal adoption of global sensitivity analysis (GSA). This methodological gap limits the ability of the RO field to critically examine its own models and to support its decision-making. The paper concludes by outlining concrete recommendations for how to effectively incorporate GSA into future RO studies in order to enhance model transparency, relevance, and credibility.
